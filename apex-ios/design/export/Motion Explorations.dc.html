1Motion and haptics · what moves, how, and what you feel

Owner: smooth, never jerky; crossfades sometimes, bounces sometimes; cover haptics too; notes plus a live page. Every app-owned moment from the screens designed so far, each with two treatments to compare. The green chip shows where a haptic would play and which one.

Springs here are approximated with CSS curves; the build uses SwiftUI’s smooth and snappy springs. Reduce Motion (switch below) shows what each becomes with that accessibility setting on. Full list per screen in motion-decisions.md.

System-owned · not ours to design
Push and back between screens · sheets opening and closing · tab switches · the keyboard · picker wheels and the inline calendar (with their own selection haptics) · confirmation dialogs · ••• menus · large-title collapse and the scroll edge · glass button press. Use the system versions so they match the rest of iOS 27 and follow Reduce Motion.
Already locked
Home: cards Rise on load (12px, 420ms, 55ms stagger); check-in slides in last then one pulse; Save collapses the check-in (320ms); header collapses over 56px. Calendar: months slide by direction (28px, 260ms); a tapped day’s card fades in (240ms). Glow: one 900ms pulse for something newly due.
Proposed rules
Three timings: quick (150–200ms, color and state), smooth (300–350ms spring, no bounce, anything that moves or resizes), snappy (about 300ms, small bounce, only things you place or tick yourself). Content arriving never bounces. Reduce Motion: slides and bounces become fades, no stagger. Haptics follow Apple’s meanings and stay rare.
